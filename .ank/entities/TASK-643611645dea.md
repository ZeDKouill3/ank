The screen itself: what the three tasks before it built, joined and drawn. It waits on the data (READS and the Dag), the layout (graph.rs) and the slot (Focus::Graph), and on nothing else.

**What is shown by default, and why.** Measured on this corpus on 2026-10-14: 501 tasks, of which 470 done, 19 closed, 12 open; 260 tasks carry no edge at all. A graph that drew all of it would open on five hundred rows of history, which is the list ADR-559eebf5c6f5 refused when it said what the reader shows first is the work that is alive. So the default is the open work and the blockers it directly names, whatever their status -- a finished blocker is what makes a task Ready, and a reader who sees Ready should be able to see why. The budget this spends is the human reader's, which is where ADR-3e6ce108edcd puts it: the CLI answers the program with every row, and the screen decides what a person is handed first. Reaching the rest is the next task.

**Readiness is a character and not a word.** The shared table (ADR-1f70ce2c3eac, ank_contract::meaning) names open, in_progress, done and closed, and its own suite asserts that blocked is not a status. A coloured word the table does not know would be the second table that decision forbids. So the status column keeps the word the CLI gave, and what the DAG adds -- ready, waiting -- is the node glyph on the lane, in characters, as ADR-559eebf5c6f5 requires of anything a person has to read without colour.

**Spawns.** Moving a cursor spawns nothing (ADR-ac6be1ebe9aa makes it explicit, ADR-0bb7ea8991bc is why): the graph is one read, taken when the panel is first needed and on u, and nothing in a repaint reaches it. The claims and queue already follow this pattern in App::reload_rest.

**Scale.** A frame of the whole graph is a few hundred rows and about a dozen lanes; the layout is recomputed when the Dag, the fold or the search moves and not per frame -- the listing is frame-scoped (TASK-def2cadf48b1) but the layout is not a filter, and the view may hold it beside the Dag it was computed from.
